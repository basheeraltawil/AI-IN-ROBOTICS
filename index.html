About the Book

This website offers a glimpse into the world of intelligent robotics, drawing on insights from the second edition of "Artificial Intelligence for Robotics" by Francis X. Govers III. The book serves as a practical guide for robotics engineers and enthusiasts who are ready to advance their skills by applying Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) techniques to real-world robot tasks.

The core objective of the book is to empower you with essential skills to efficiently operate robots using advanced AI/ML techniques such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), computer vision, object recognition, genetic algorithms, and reinforcement learning.

Core Concepts and Fundamental Lessons

Module 1: Building Blocks for Robotics and AI

The AI Robot Defined

Unlike regular robots programmed for pre-defined behaviors, an AI robot's software is designed to make decisions, learn, and adapt to its environment based on data from its sensors.

When to Use AI

Employ AI/ML when you need your robot to operate robustly in a complex, dynamic real-world environment. Ideal for problems difficult or impossible to solve with traditional programming.

AI Development History

Many AI techniques were developed in the 1960s-70s. Their resurgence is due to faster computers, cheaper storage, and access to vast data through the internet.

Narrow vs. General AI

The book focuses on narrow AI, which simulates thinking for specific applications, rather than general AI (a full artificial intelligent brain).

Module 4: Conclusions and Reflections

Project Completion

Set specific, measurable goals to determine when a robot design project is finished, avoiding endless "tinkering".

Careers in Robotics

Robotics is highly interdisciplinary, requiring skills from mechanical and electrical engineering to software development and psychology.

AI Reality Check

The author dismisses fears of AI taking over as exaggerated. AI systems currently lack "needs" or "ambitions" that would drive them to self-perpetuate.

Understanding AI Risk

AI systems are inherently non-deterministic and operate on probabilities. This makes them challenging for safety-critical applications without robust risk mitigation.
